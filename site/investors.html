What this says

The failing bearing raises a sustained alarm ~2.5 days before it dies and ~2 days before the earliest neighbour, with the correct element named and no training data. All four bearings share a shaft and an identical BPFO, so the neighbours alarm late from casing crosstalk — the claim is lead time, not neighbour silence. Every number here is reproducible from the public repo with pytest tests/.

What the evidence does not support

Stated up front, because you would find it in the repo anyway

  • No false-alarm rate. One healthy record, reading 3.95 against a warn threshold of 4.0 — a 1.2% margin. A rate needs a healthy population, not one capture.
  • No lead-time distribution. n = 1 failure event, so 2.5 days has no lower bound — and nothing here supports the word weeks.
  • Ball faults are unsolved. Detected as a level change, but the element ranking is wrong. Race faults, the majority of failures, are solid.
  • No per-bearing localisation on a shared shaft.
  • Nothing yet through the product's own sensor chain — CWRU and IMS are lab piezo recordings at constant speed and load.

Closing these needs weeks of work and almost no capex: IMS sets 1 and 3 are already downloaded, and XJTU-SY, PRONOSTIA/FEMTO, Paderborn and MFPT would take the run-to-failure count from 1 to roughly 35.

Where the value actually accrues

Not the algorithm

Envelope analysis is public-domain literature — Balderston (Boeing, 1969), Darlow & Badgley (1974), McFadden & Smith (1984), Antoni & Randall spectral kurtosis (2006–07). The patent that inspired this project, US 7,599,804 B2 (FAG Kugelfischer, now Schaeffler), lapsed in 2013. Full provenance is documented in the repo.

What compounds is local presence, per-plant failure data, and the maintenance relationships — not the DSP. We would rather say that than claim a technological moat that does not exist.
